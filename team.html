The people
behind the map.

Four teammates. One shared project. Meet the University of Utah senior capstone team connecting retail spaces with the information people need.

Will Black

Map editor · Human-centered design

Will Black is a member of the ShelfWise senior capstone team at the University of Utah and the originator of the project idea. He brings experience in human-centered design to a project centered on making store information easier to understand. His primary responsibility is the map editor, where managers create and organize the spaces that employees and shoppers use as a visual guide.

Will’s work covers the editor’s design and layout tools, information tools, and file input and output. He also contributes to the client-side controllers that communicate with the backend through the Fetch API. This connects the experience of editing a store layout with the data that supports it. Beyond implementation, his responsibilities include refining the project design and contributing to the design document and code-style decisions. Through this combination of interface work and project planning, Will helps translate ShelfWise’s original concept into an editor built around the needs of the people using it.

Conner Francis

Backend systems · Client–server communication

Conner Francis is a member of the ShelfWise senior capstone team at the University of Utah. His experience in networking, particularly client–server communication and distributed systems, provides a foundation for the application’s backend routing and request handling. He has also completed coursework in web development covering backend implementation and frontend integration, connecting his networking background with the practical structure of a web application.

On ShelfWise, Conner focuses on routing, controller logic, and both server-side and client-side request handling. These responsibilities sit between the interface people interact with and the information the system stores and returns. He also contributes to frontend user-input forms, helping connect the collection of information with the backend processes that handle it. His role supports the communication needed when a user searches for a product, requests store information, or submits an update. By working across these boundaries, Conner contributes to the application’s shared flow of data and to the coordination between frontend and backend components.

Bradley Lund

Data infrastructure · Authentication and security

Bradley Lund is a member of the ShelfWise senior capstone team at the University of Utah. He brings experience in web development, particularly backend management and form handling, together with coursework in cybersecurity. His work on the project focuses on the infrastructure that supports the store map and the information associated with it, connecting the application’s visible interface with its underlying data systems.

Bradley’s responsibilities include database management and handling, website hosting, and authentication and security. These areas support the storage of store layouts and product information, the delivery of the application, and the distinction between users with different permissions. He also contributes to the underlying models used by the map editor, helping connect editing operations with the application’s representation of store data. His role combines backend implementation with attention to how information is managed and accessed. Within the ShelfWise team, this work provides a foundation for the frontend and editor features that make store organization visible to shoppers, employees, and managers.

Yi Yang

Frontend development · User experience

Yi Yang is a member of the ShelfWise senior capstone team at the University of Utah. His experience in interface design and frontend development centers on clear, user-friendly presentation. On ShelfWise, he supports both the web and mobile frontends, with particular attention to how store and product information reaches the people using the application. His role connects the project’s underlying data with the experience of reading and navigating that information.

Yi’s responsibilities include designing and implementing information displays, maintaining interface consistency, and integrating APIs for frontend data presentation. These areas contribute to a shared experience across views and platforms, helping users understand the relationship between a store map and the information attached to it. He also explores AI-supported features as part of the project’s development work. His contribution focuses on the point where technical functionality becomes something a shopper, employee, or manager can use: finding relevant information, understanding the interface, and moving between related tasks without losing context.
